# Lower published values on the same structures (stated for the criterion's comparison clause)

The leaderboard has a second task for this element, `mlearnall_Si_forces` (https://pages.nist.gov/jarvis_leaderboard/AI/MLFF/mlearnall_Si_forces/, page sha256 e1db5f72e400e6d2a4491f8f67e289d6a25d41b191a72b674f26dffa50f0c64a). It has an EMPTY train set and its test set is all 239 structures of the mlearn Si data (train and test ids of `mlearn_Si_forces` together); it scores models zero-shot.
Its best entry is `mlearn_analysis_Si_eqV2_153M_omat` (listed 2024-11-22) with force MAE 0.012335 eV/A on all 239 structures. Restricting that entry's published predictions to the 25 official test ids of `mlearn_Si_forces` gives 0.013515 eV/A (script: `compare_mlearnall.py`, standard library only, fetches from raw.githubusercontent.com).
This package's ensemble gives 0.049536 eV/A on the same 25 test ids, i.e. HIGHER than that entry by a factor of 3.7.

How this relates to the claim: the model named above is a pretrained universal potential (an eqV2 model trained on large public datasets; the model name lists omat / mp / salex). It was not trained only on this element's train ids, so it does not meet the training clause of the success criterion and is not an entry of the task `mlearn_Si_forces` that the claim is made on. We did not verify its training data. The claim is therefore the criterion for `mlearn_Si_forces` (a model trained from scratch on the train ids only, at least 5% below the best listed value on that task's page). It is NOT a claim that this is the lowest force MAE known for these structures.
