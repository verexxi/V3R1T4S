# Lower published values on the same structures (stated for the criterion's comparison clause)

The leaderboard has a second task for this element, `mlearnall_Cu_forces` (https://pages.nist.gov/jarvis_leaderboard/AI/MLFF/mlearnall_Cu_forces/, page sha256 01d27bc1fe8c7fa3c7b353cfa2c93f814dae3950530c8eda6af132906472e521). It has an EMPTY train set and its test set is all 293 structures of the mlearn Cu data (train and test ids of `mlearn_Cu_forces` together); it scores models zero-shot.
Its best entry is `mlearn_analysis_Cu_eqV2_86M_omat_mp_salex` (listed 2024-11-22) with force MAE 0.004834 eV/A on all 293 structures. Restricting that entry's published predictions to the 31 official test ids of `mlearn_Cu_forces` gives 0.005205 eV/A (script: `compare_mlearnall.py`, standard library only, fetches from raw.githubusercontent.com).
This package's ensemble gives 0.008574 eV/A on the same 31 test ids, i.e. HIGHER than that entry by a factor of 1.6.

How this relates to the claim: the model named above is a pretrained universal potential (an eqV2 model trained on large public datasets; the model name lists omat / mp / salex). It was not trained only on this element's train ids, so it does not meet the training clause of the success criterion and is not an entry of the task `mlearn_Cu_forces` that the claim is made on. We did not verify its training data. The claim is therefore the criterion for `mlearn_Cu_forces` (a model trained from scratch on the train ids only, at least 5% below the best listed value on that task's page). It is NOT a claim that this is the lowest force MAE known for these structures.
