Brief: beat the best listed Cu force MAE (matgl_mlearn, 0.017749 eV/A) on the 31 official mlearn_Cu_forces test structures by at least 5%, from the 262 train structures only. Cu cells hold up to 107 atoms (about 2.7x slower than Ge or Si), so only one width was run. The metric reproduction and the val rule (every tenth train id) are the ones built for the Mo package. The recipe (batch size 3, learning rate 0.005, 150 epochs, 2 interaction layers, r_max 4.5) was fixed on Mo. Before any Ni or Cu result was read, PREREG.v4 declared the 16-channel width only for Ni and Cu (compute), that the three final seeds could start before the validation run ended, and that the validation run was a sanity gate. The Cu validation run finished after the finals: val MAE 0.008357 (gate 0.01686). Three seeds on all 262 train ids gave test MAEs of 0.008758, 0.009043 and 0.008793 (sd 0.00016), ensemble 0.008574, 51.7% below the live best and below the 0.0179 that a later paper (ALIGNN 2.0, unverified there) lists. A pretrained zero-shot eqV2 entry on the sibling task mlearnall_Cu_forces is lower on the same test ids (0.005205), see the comparison section. What did not happen: no width selection for Cu, no linear ACE, MTP or kernel model, and no energy or stress claim.
